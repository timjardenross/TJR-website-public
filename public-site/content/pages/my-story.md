## My story

My pain began when I was 12, with scoliosis. I missed a lot of school, I struggled to connect with other kids, and I was labelled "weak". I spent years feeling confused, misdiagnosed and unheard.

In 2016 I had the first of four spinal surgeries. The first was stopped when the monitoring of my spinal cord stopped working and staff suspected a problem. The second dealt with infections I picked up in hospital. The third completed the implants, and cement was used in the fourth because the screws were coming loose. I spent 12 days in intensive care. Years later the implants broke and had to be replaced, and the wound took months to heal.

Alongside the physical pain came depression and anxiety. I also remember staff discussing my case without including me, and friends who couldn't understand what constant pain does to a life.

I've tried many things. Chinese medicine and yoga gave me short-term relief. Meditation, journaling and time away helped in other ways. Consistency has always been hard, and so has speaking up for myself in the medical system, for fear of being dismissed.

I'm writing this recovering from injections and nerve treatments, and I don't yet know if they've helped. The pain is bad right now. That is part of the story too.

I work in operational resilience in a corporate role: preparing for disruption and recovering when things go wrong. Being made redundant gave me time to step back and heal. Right now I'm focusing on advocacy and on helping others, alongside that work.

If any of this sounds like you, you're not alone, and you're not doing it wrong. This site is for people who want to be heard, and to be better prepared to speak up.

*Written November 2026. I review this page each year.*
