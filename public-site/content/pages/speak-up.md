## Being heard is a skill you can prepare for

If you live with persistent pain, you may have felt dismissed, rushed, or talked about instead of talked with. That is not a sign that you are doing it wrong. These pages help you prepare, so more of what matters to you gets said, and heard.

## Before, during and after

- **Before:** put your pain story and your questions on paper.
- **In the appointment:** share what matters to you, and ask until you understand.
- **After:** write down what was agreed, and what happens next.

Read the full guide: [How to be heard in an appointment](/how-to-be-heard).

## Tools on this site

- [Map your pain story](/pain/understand): put where, what, when and how pain affects you into words, not just a number.
- [Find the right help](/pain/care): who can help with what, and questions worth holding in mind.
- [Manage your capacity](/pain/capacity): think through what you can do on a good, a middling and a very hard day.
- [Prepare for flares](/pain/flare): build a Flare Card for difficult pain days.
- [My Pain Plan](/pain/plan): bring it together, then save, print or export it.

What you enter in these tools stays in your browser session. It is only saved on your device if you choose to save it.

## Your rights

Whatever the appointment, you have healthcare rights in Australia. The Australian Charter of Healthcare Rights lists seven: access, safety, respect, partnership, information, privacy, and the right to give feedback. [Read the Charter and what it means for you](https://www.safetyandquality.gov.au/your-rights/understanding-your-healthcare-rights).

## A clear boundary

This is information to help you prepare for a conversation. It is not medical advice, and it cannot make decisions for you. Talk with your GP or another qualified health professional about your situation.
